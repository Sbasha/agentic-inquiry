# Openings and moments

An opening should place the reader inside the real subject without throat-clearing.
It does not need to shock, threaten, confess, or open an artificial loop.

## Possible openings

- a confirmed moment already carrying the story's question;
- a concrete observation whose significance becomes clearer later;
- a plain statement of where or when the story begins;
- an image that returns naturally near the end;
- a line of verified or carefully qualified remembered dialogue;
- a brief reflection that does not give away a manufactured moral.

Choose the opening that requires the least invented context.
Do not use personal hardship, identity, vulnerability, another person, or confidential detail merely because it creates attention.

## Key moments

A key moment may be an event, choice, realization, interruption, consequence, silence, joke, or later reinterpretation.
It does not need to surprise the reader or demand action.

Check:

- Is the moment confirmed by the source material?
- Does its placement preserve chronology and causation?
- Is the detail necessary for the story rather than decorative?
- Does it expose another person or cross a disclosure boundary?
- Has pacing been slowed because the moment warrants attention, or only to stage drama?

## Turns and endings

Some stories contain a clear turn.
Others accumulate meaning or remain unresolved.
Do not add a conversion, breakthrough, failure, or lesson when the material does not contain one.

An ending may land on consequence, aftermath, reflection, an image, a question, or an honest lack of resolution.
Do not restate the story, announce its significance, or convert it into a call to action by default.
