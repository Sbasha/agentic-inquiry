# Story lenses

Use a lens only after confirming that the material is a story.
The default is no named framework.
Minto, Pyramid Principle, and SCQA structure arguments and expository documents, not stories.

## Simple chronology

Follow events in the order experienced.
Use when sequence itself supplies clarity or emotional movement.

Risk: including every event because it happened.
Keep only events that belong to the chosen narrative center.

## Scene and reflection

Move between a bounded scene and what the narrator understands now.
Use when the value lies in both the moment and later interpretation.

Risk: explaining the scene until it becomes an illustration of a packaged lesson.

## Controlled nonlinearity

Open with a later image or consequence, return to the earlier sequence, then reconnect without changing causation.
Use when the supplied material has a natural echo or unresolved question.

Risk: withholding information only to manufacture suspense.

## Three-act lens

Use setup, sustained change or difficulty, and aftermath when the material genuinely contains those movements.

Risk: inventing conflict, escalation, or resolution to fill the shape.

## Causal chain

Use a sequence of events in which each event materially caused or constrained the next.
This is useful for incident stories, migrations, experiments, and project histories.

Risk: treating chronology as causation.
Mark uncertainty when the link is an inference.

## Episodic or braided story

Place several moments beside each other because their relationship produces meaning.
Use when no single transformation contains the whole piece.

Risk: forcing a neat conclusion over material that remains plural or unresolved.

## Observational story

Let attention, description, juxtaposition, and reaction carry the piece.
Use for cultural, place, craft, and personal observations that do not need a plot.

Risk: adding an argument, moral, or professional analogy because the story feels quiet.

## Selection test

Choose the lens that preserves the confirmed material with the least invention.
If no lens improves the sequence, do not name one.
If two lenses create materially different meanings, present both briefly and ask the user to choose.
