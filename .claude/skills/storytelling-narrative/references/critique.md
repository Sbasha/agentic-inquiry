# Story critique

Review the story at the reader's intended pace before changing it.
Name what is working before proposing cuts or reordering.

## Form and center

- Is the piece actually a story rather than an argument with an anecdote?
- What movement, moment, relationship, or pattern of attention holds it together?
- Does the center belong to the user, or was it inferred by the editor?
- Did the piece drift into a different moral or professional lesson?

## Source integrity

- Which events, details, quotations, emotions, and causal links are confirmed?
- Are memory and chronology qualified where needed?
- Did reordering imply knowledge, blame, or causation that did not exist at the time?
- Did the draft invent sensory detail, dialogue, conflict, stakes, or resolution?

## People and disclosure

- Does the story expose another person or use them as a device?
- Is personal detail necessary for meaning, or present because it increases intimacy?
- Would removing the most dramatic detail leave a worthwhile story?
- Has this exact use cleared the owning disclosure boundary?

## Movement and pacing

- Does each scene, observation, or reflection belong?
- Is setup longer than the reader needs?
- Are quiet passages being mistaken for defects merely because they are quiet?
- Do fragments, short paragraphs, repetition, and withheld information feel natural or staged?
- Does the ending complete the movement without summarizing or packaging a lesson?

## Register

- Does the prose sound like the source material rather than a generic story voice?
- Were humor, excitement, irritation, uncertainty, or understatement preserved?
- Did editing add lyricism, sentiment, mystery, confidence, or intimacy?
- Does a technical story retain exact system facts and a cultural story retain the actual object of attention?

## Reframe boundary

Changing the narrative center, event selection, point of view, causal account, person centered, stakes, or meaning is a reframe.
Present that proposal to the user before silently rebuilding the story.

Run `rewrite-slop` only after the story structure and disclosure are settled.
The surface pass must not move events, flatten fragments, or add a thesis-first opening.
